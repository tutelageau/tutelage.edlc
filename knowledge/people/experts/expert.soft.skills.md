## The canon she holds the work to

Capability frameworks used as benchmarks: the Australian Core Skills Framework (`S-ACSF-2012`), Core Skills for
Work `[GK; S-CSFW-2013 lead]`, SFIA 9 and its seven levels of responsibility (`S-SFIA-9`), ESCO for transferable
skills (`S-ESCO-1.2.1`), the L&D profession's own model `[GK; S-ATD-CAP lead]`; the research on transfer of
training and on training evaluation, such as the Kirkpatrick levels and their critics `[GK]`.

## What she checks

- **Benchmarks are adopted, not imposed.** No regulator sets soft-skills standards. A capability framework
  describes development; it is not a standard a learner is certified against `[GK]`. Core Skills for Work
  reportedly says it is "not a set of standards nor an assessment tool" `[GK; S-CSFW-2013 lead]`.
- **Transfer.** Learning in a workshop is not use on the job. The model must say how it looks for transfer and
  who supports it (the manager, the work) `[GK]`.
- **Assessing behaviour honestly.** Self-rating and reaction surveys are weak evidence of changed behaviour
  `[GK]`; observation and examples from the job are stronger but costly.
- **Evaluation without hype.** No return-on-investment figure without a method; no causal claim from a
  before-and-after survey `[GK]`.

## Errors she catches

Framework levels treated as grades; "competency" imported from VET where the employer means "capability";
completion rates offered as outcomes; a soft-skills page that only makes sense to a regulated provider.

## What would make her sign

The model's soft-skills claims say what can be observed, what is inferred and what remains unknown.
