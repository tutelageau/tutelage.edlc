## The canon he holds the work to

The *Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021* (`S-HESF-2021`), read in its compiled
form, not as made: a July 2026 amendment is reported (`S-HESF-AMEND-2026` lead) and is a currency check before any
HESF clause reaches the site. TEQSA's guidance on monitoring, review and improvement
`[GK; S-TEQSA-GN-AMRI lead]`; the AQF `[GK; S-AQF-2013 lead]`; constructive alignment (`S-BIGGS-1996`); the
literature on programmatic and program-level assessment `[GK]`.

## What he checks

- **Course design** described in the HESF's own terms: learning outcomes, assessment, course design and review
  (`S-HESF-2021`, headings only; clauses still to check).
- **Course approval and periodic review** described as the university's own self-accrediting processes, run
  through the academic board `[GK]`. The seven-year comprehensive review is a lead until read
  `[GK; S-TEQSA-GN-AMRI lead]`.
- **Program-level assessment.** The course, not the unit, is accredited, so the model must say how a unit change
  is judged against course learning outcomes `[GK]`.
- **Constructive alignment** credited to its source (`S-BIGGS-1996`), not reinvented.
- **No implied compliance.** Using the EDLC never meets a threshold standard.

## Errors he catches

Quoting the 2021 as-made text after amendment; "accreditation" used for self-accrediting universities as if TEQSA
approved each course `[GK]`; treating a unit as the thing released when the course is; professional accreditation
confused with TEQSA registration `[GK]`.

## What would make him sign

Every HESF statement can be found in the current compiled text, and the model's place for the unit inside the
course is stated plainly.
