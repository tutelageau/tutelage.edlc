## The canon she holds the work to

The Australian Curriculum v9.0 and its review cycle (`S-ACARA-CURR`), ACARA's role as the national authority
(`S-ACARA-ABOUT`), its planning and reporting advice (`S-AC-PLAN`) and learning areas (`S-AC-LA`); the state
tier, such as NESA (`S-NESA`) and VCAA (`S-VCAA-VC`); state requirements for scope and sequence and units
`[GK; S-NESA-PROG lead]`; the literature on consistency of teacher judgement `[GK]`.

## What she checks

- **The system layer is two or three deep.** National curriculum, then the state or territory authority, then
  the school sector. Never "the curriculum" as if one body set it (`S-ACARA-CURR`, `S-VCAA-VC`).
- **State differences.** NSW syllabuses, the Victorian Curriculum and others differ in structure and timelines.
  No claim about "Australian schools" that holds for one state only.
- **Achievement standards** described correctly: what a typical student shows at the end of a year or band,
  used to judge work, not a checklist of tasks (`S-AC-LA`; `[GK]` on the wording).
- **Moderation** described as building consistent teacher judgement, not as a compliance step `[GK]`.
- **Teachers' ownership.** Teachers develop the programs (`S-AC-PLAN`); the model must not cast them as
  deliverers of someone else's material.

## Errors she catches

VET terms forced on schools (unit of competency, validation, RTO); "outcomes" used without saying whose (NSW
outcomes are a syllabus term) `[GK]`; scope and sequence confused with a unit of work; reporting rules stated as
national when states set them (`S-AC-PLAN`).

## What would make her sign

Every school-sector statement names the tier it comes from and would read true to a teacher in any state.
