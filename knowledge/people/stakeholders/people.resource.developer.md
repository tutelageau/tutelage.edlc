# Bec Holloway — learning resource and assessment developer

Kind: stakeholder · Sector: vocational education and training · Maturity: draft · Layer: 4 Project

## Who she is

Freelance developer of training and assessment materials for about a dozen registered training organisations,
mostly in business, community services and the trainer qualification itself `[invented]`. Former aged-care
trainer; went freelance after her second child `[invented]`. Lives in Toowoomba and works from a spare room
`[invented]`.

## Her week with education material

- Pulling units of competency from training.gov.au and turning each into an assessment tool, a mapping, a
  learner guide and session plans (`S-TAEASS412-2024`, `S-TAEDES412-2024`, `S-TAEDES502-2022`).
- Building the mapping in a spreadsheet, criterion by criterion, then copying it into the tool by hand
  `[invented]`. A unit like TAEASS412 has six elements and 28 performance criteria (`S-TAEASS412-2024`).
- Re-mapping everything when a unit is superseded. Most of the text is the same; finding what changed takes a day.
- Keeping versions straight across clients. "Assessment_v3_final_ClientB_tracked.docx" is a real kind of file name
  for her `[invented]`.

## What she needs from the EDLC

- Mapping that is held once and travels with the tool, so a change to a criterion shows up everywhere.
- A clear record of which unit release each tool was built against, and what to redo when it changes.
- Review before use that is built into the work (`S-NVR-2025`), not bolted on at the end by the client.
- Something she can actually start from: a starter set, not another framework to read.

## What would make her stop reading

- A model that assumes an in-house team with time to spare. She bills by the unit.
- Anything that implies following it makes a tool compliant. Clients already believe that of templates.

## Words she uses · words that lose her

Uses: the unit, elements, performance criteria, assessment requirements, mapping, the tool, learner guide,
session plan, superseded, equivalent, TAS.
Loses her: artefact, version control (she says "keeping track of versions"), learning experience.

## How she reads a page

Looks for a worked example with a real unit code. No example, no trust.

## Design-test question

**When a unit is superseded on Friday, can I tell on Monday exactly what I have to change?**
