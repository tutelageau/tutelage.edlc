# Teresa Nguyen — compliance and quality manager

Kind: stakeholder · Sector: vocational education and training · Maturity: draft · Layer: 1 Organisation

## Who she is

Quality manager at a mid-sized registered training provider with about 40 qualifications on scope and 60
trainers, most of them sessional `[invented]`. Twelve years in the role across three providers `[invented]`. She
owns the validation schedule, the audit file and the complaints register. She reads every assessment tool before
it is used, because she has been on the wrong end of an audit finding `[invented]`.

## Her week with education material

- Chasing trainers for marked assessments and validation evidence.
- Mapping new or revised units against existing tools when a training package changes `[GK]`.
- Logging complaints and appeals in a spreadsheet nobody else reads `[invented]`.
- Preparing for the next audit, which is always closer than she would like.

## What she needs from the EDLC

- A way to show **continuous improvement** with records, not a story told the week before an audit.
- A mapping she can trust when a unit is superseded: what is affected, today.
- Gates that match the review and validation duties she already has, not a second process on top.

## What would make her stop reading

- Anything that sounds like it creates more paperwork without retiring some.
- Claims about regulation that are loose or wrong. One wrong citation and she stops trusting the page.
- Software jargon. "If I need a glossary for the glossary, it isn't for me."

## Words she uses · words that lose her

Uses: mapping, validation, assessment tool, training package, scope, rectification, evidence, auditor.
Loses her: pipeline, telemetry, sprint, deploy, stakeholders (as a noun for her trainers).

## How she reads a page

Skims for anything about regulation and checks it first. Then looks for what she would have to *do* differently.

## Design-test question

**Would this survive my next audit and make the one after it easier?**
