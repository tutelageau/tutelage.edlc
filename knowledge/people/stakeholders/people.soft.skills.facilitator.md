Kind: stakeholder · Sector: workplace learning and soft skills · Maturity: draft · Layer: 3 Team, 4 Program

## Who he is

Designs and runs leadership and communication programs, half the year on contract to a mid-size engineering
company and half to whoever else calls `[invented]`. Former hospitality manager from Brisbane who moved into
learning and development fifteen years ago `[invented]`. Works with one other contractor on most programs
`[invented]`.

## His year with education material

- Writing a leadership program of four workshops and coaching calls, with a facilitator guide and workbook
  `[invented]`.
- Choosing a capability framework to describe what the program builds, because the client asks for one. Nothing
  makes him use it; the company adopts it (`S-ACSF-2012`, `S-SFIA-9`; `[GK; S-CSFW-2013 lead]`).
- Collecting feedback forms that say "great session" and tell him nothing about whether anyone leads differently. `[invented]`
- Rewriting a module after every run because the last group was different `[invented]`.

## What he needs from the EDLC

- An honest way to answer "how do we know it worked?" that is better than smile sheets and cheaper than a study.
- A sense of when a program is good enough to stop rewriting.
- Gates light enough for unregulated work, where the benchmark is chosen, not imposed.

## What would make him stop reading

- Pages full of units of competency and regulators. None apply to him.
- Promises of measurable behaviour change. He has heard them sold, and seen them not delivered.
- Anything that turns a room of people into data points.

## Words he uses · words that lose him

Uses: program, workshop, cohort, capability, behaviour, transfer, application on the job, debrief, the client,
the business.
Loses him: assessment (he says "check-in" or "observation"), validation, training package.

## How he reads a page

Reads for the part about programs like his. Then for the part on evaluation, which he reads twice.

## Design-test question

**Would this help me show a client their people lead differently, without pretending I can prove it?**
