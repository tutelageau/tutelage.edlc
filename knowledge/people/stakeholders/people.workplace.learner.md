# Jordan Ellis — newly promoted team leader

Kind: stakeholder · Sector: workplace learning and soft skills (as a learner) · Maturity: draft · Layer: 4 Program

## Who they are

Promoted four months ago to lead a team of eight in a customer service centre, after five years on the phones
`[invented]`. Grew up in Darwin; first person in their family in a management role `[invented]`. Enrolled in the
company's twelve-week leadership program, alongside the full job `[invented]`. Uses they/them `[invented]`.

## Their weeks with education material

- A half-day workshop every three weeks, then a workbook to fill in "in your own time", which does not exist
  `[invented]`.
- Trying to use a feedback model from the workshop on a real team member the next morning. It works once, then
  not `[invented]`.
- Online modules that feel like they were written for someone in a different company. `[invented]`
- Being asked to rate the session out of five before they have tried any of it. `[invented]`

## What they need from the EDLC

- Material that fits the job they actually do, with examples from a team like theirs.
- A chance to say, weeks later, what worked and what did not, and to see it change something.
- Learning that fits around rosters, not on top of them.

## What would make them stop reading

- Anything that talks about learners as numbers or engagement.
- Long pages. They read in breaks of ten minutes.
- Corporate buzzwords that sound like the program's own brochure.

## Words they use · words that lose them

Uses: the program, the workshop, my team, my manager, feedback, the workbook, actually using it.
Loses them: capability framework, transfer of learning, cohort, outcomes, evaluation.

## How they read a page

Looks for whether anyone asked a learner. If learners only appear as survey scores, they are gone.

## Design-test question

**Would this make the next group's program more useful on the floor than mine was?**
