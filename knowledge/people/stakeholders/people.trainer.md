# Mick Rawlins — trainer and assessor

Kind: stakeholder · Sector: vocational education and training · Maturity: draft · Layer: 4 Project

## Who he is

Ex-electrician, teaching apprentices for nine years, part-time at two providers `[invented]`. Delivers mostly
face-to-face in a workshop, some on-site visits. Marks a lot of assessments, often late at night `[invented]`.
Respected by learners because he "has done the job".

## His week with education material

- Teaching from learner guides he did not write and often supplements with his own handouts `[invented]`.
- Skipping sections that do not match how the work is done now, and filling gaps on the whiteboard.
- Marking against marking guides that sometimes do not match the task as written.
- Telling the quality manager about problems in the corridor. They rarely get written down.

## What he needs from the EDLC

- Somewhere his fixes and complaints about the materials actually go, and come back changed.
- Materials and assessments that agree with each other, so he is not explaining the gap to learners.
- Recognition that the same course runs differently in his workshop than online.

## What would make him stop reading

- Anything that treats trainers as a variable to be measured.
- More forms. "Don't make me log it in three places."
- Long paragraphs. He reads on his phone between classes.

## Words he uses · words that lose him

Uses: the book, the assessment, the marking guide, my apprentices, the job, the unit.
Loses him: observability, outcome descriptor (he says "what they need to be able to do"), stakeholder.

## How he reads a page

Looks for himself in it. If trainers only appear as data sources, he is gone.

## Design-test question

**Does this make my Monday class better, or just my paperwork longer?**
