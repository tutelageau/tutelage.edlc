<h2 id="traceability">Traceability</h2>

Every artefact links to the outcomes it serves: assessment tasks to outcome descriptors, content blocks to tasks, sessions to content. Traceability runs both ways. From any outcome you can find everything that teaches and assesses it; from any artefact you can find why it exists.

This is what lets a change to one outcome be followed to every affected artefact. The mapping matrix built at [Architect](/stages/architect/) is its backbone.

<h2 id="versioning">Versioning</h2>

A program's history should be recoverable: what the standard said on a given date, what changed between releases, and which version each cohort received. Keeping a dated record of every change, rather than overwriting the last version, makes all three answerable.

<h2 id="dependencies">When the standard changes</h2>

Every program depends on the standard it was built against. When an external body revises or supersedes that standard, every program built on it is affected. The EDLC handles a revision in four steps:

1. Identify what changed between the old and the new standard.
2. Find the affected artefacts through the traceability links.
3. Assess the impact on each.
4. Route the work back through the gates.

<h2 id="observability">Observability</h2>

Observability means collecting evidence about how the program performs, not only about how learners perform. Candidate signals, each tied to outcome descriptors:

- Pass, resubmission and appeal rates per assessment item.
- Points in the sequence where learners stall or withdraw.
- Actual time on task compared with the planned session time.
- Trainer and assessor notes on what they skipped, reworked or supplemented.
- Learner feedback tied to specific modules, not only to the course as a whole.

Open specifications such as the Experience API (xAPI) provide a common format for activity data. Learning analytics research offers methods, along with well-documented cautions about privacy and over-interpretation. See the [guardrails](/guardrails/).

<h2 id="curriculum-debt">Curriculum debt</h2>

Curriculum debt is the accumulated cost of expedient decisions in a learning program. Examples stop reflecting practice and patches pile on patches. Assessments are kept because they are compliant rather than good, and materials still target a cohort the institution no longer serves.

Curriculum debt is not always bad. Taking it on can be a reasonable trade to meet a start date. It becomes harmful when it is invisible. Candidate indicators:

- Time since the last substantive review.
- Number of unreviewed patches.
- Complaint and appeal counts.
- Divergence between the planned and the delivered program.

Whether these indicators can be combined into a measure that predicts problems is one of the claims on the [research agenda](/research/).
