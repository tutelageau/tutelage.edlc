This page says, for five settings, where the learning standard comes from, which of that setting's own words the EDLC's words translate to, and which of the ten gates that setting's rules already supply. Every fact about a sector links to its source; where nothing has been verified, the page says so rather than guessing.

The paper's own grounding is Australian vocational education. Whether the model transfers to the other four settings is claim 6 on the [research agenda](/research/), untested.

## Schools

The standard comes from above the school. The Australian Curriculum version 9.0 was approved on 1 April 2022 and is reviewed on a six-year cycle. States and territories implement it on their own timelines ([ACARA, *Curriculum*](https://www.acara.edu.au/curriculum)).

In New South Wales, NESA sets syllabuses for all schools in the state ([NSW Government, *NESA*](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa)). In Victoria, the VCAA's curriculum guidelines are supplementary to the advice of the school sector authorities ([VCAA, *Victorian Curriculum F–10*](https://www.vcaa.vic.edu.au/curriculum/foundation-10/Pages/default.aspx)).

So the standard a school designs against is national, state and sector-shaped at once. The state's timeline, not the national one, is the calendar the loop runs on.

In this sector's words: the curriculum has eight learning areas. Its content descriptions and achievement standards are "the focus for planning, programming, teaching and assessment" ([ACARA, *Learning areas*](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/help/learning-areas)). Teachers develop teaching and learning programs, and states and territories set reporting ([ACARA, *Planning, teaching, assessing and reporting*](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/help/f-10-curriculum-overview/planning--teaching--assessing-and-reporting)).

Read "learning standard" as the curriculum or the syllabus, and "program" as a teaching and learning program. The nearest words to "outcome descriptor" are content description and achievement standard.

Which gates a state's or a sector authority's requirements already supply is not yet established. This site has verified where the standard comes from, not what the state requires of a program before or after it is taught. Until that is read, treat every gate as yours to set, and the state's implementation timeline as the release calendar.

## Vocational education and training

The standard comes from the training package. A unit of competency sets out elements, performance criteria and assessment requirements, as [TAEASS412 *Assess competence*](https://training.gov.au/TrainingComponentFiles/TAE/TAEASS412_R1.pdf) shows.

Providers' rules are the *National Vocational Education and Training Regulator (Outcome Standards for Registered Training Organisations) Instrument 2025* ([Federal Register of Legislation, F2025L00354](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00354/latest/text)). It commenced on 1 July 2025.

In this sector's words: "training product", "assessment tool", "scope of registration", "validation".

Two of the ten gates are already in the instrument, under two different names. Standard 1.3(2)(b) requires that "assessment tools are reviewed prior to use": that pre-use **review** is the gate at stage 8, [Validate and release](/stages/validate-and-release/).

Standard 1.5(2)(b) requires every training product to be validated "at least once every five years". It requires validation more often where the organisation becomes aware of risks, of changes to the product, or of relevant feedback. Standard 1.5(2)(c) has risk decide which components are validated and how large a sample.

That post-use **validation** is the gate at stage 10, [Improve](/stages/improve/).

Passing either gate in the EDLC's sense is not the same as meeting the Standard, and nothing on this site is a compliance tool. The paper's own guardrail is that compliance is not quality: passing an audit shows conformance, not that learners thrived.

## Higher education

Above the provider sits the *Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021*. Its current compilation is F2026C00804 of 14 July 2026 ([Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L00488/latest/text)).

This site has read the Framework's headings, not its clauses, so it claims nothing about what they require of course design or review. Nor does it yet say where a course's learning standard sits between the Framework and the provider's own course learning outcomes. The paper's notes on Specify say only that the standard's structure can represent a university subject.

TEQSA's guidance supplies one gate. Its *Guidance note: Academic monitoring, review and improvement* expects providers to "comprehensively review all their registered courses of study … at least every 7 years" ([TEQSA](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/guidance-notes/guidance-note-academic-monitoring-review-and-improvement)). Those reviews involve external referencing and are overseen by academic governance.

That comprehensive review is a gate at stage 10, [Improve](/stages/improve/). In this sector's words: "course of study", "academic governance", "external referencing".

Where a yearly course-approval cycle and a periodic review sit in the loop is an open question. The paper plans releases against intake dates, but it was grounded in vocational education and says so. Its fit with a university's approval rhythm is the case study claim 6 proposes; where the model does not yet fit a university, this page says so too.

## Workplace and soft skills

No body above the organisation sets the standard. The organisation adopts one or writes its own. The paper's second stage, Specify, takes in *or authors* the standard, and its first principle allows a standard "authored in-house". Three published frameworks an organisation might adopt:

- the *Core Skills for Work Developmental Framework* (2013): three skill clusters, ten skill areas, five stages of performance ([DEWR](https://www.dewr.gov.au/node/8374));
- SFIA 9 (October 2024): seven levels of responsibility ([SFIA Foundation](https://sfia-online.org/en/sfia-9));
- the *Australian Core Skills Framework* (2012): five core skills ([ACER](https://people.acer.org/en/publications/australian-core-skills-framework-acsf/)).

In these frameworks' words: "skill cluster", "skill area", "stage of performance", "level of responsibility", "core skill".

No rule supplies a gate, so every gate is yours to set. The paper says they can be lighter: regulation sets how strict a gate is, not whether the stage exists.

One rule is the paper's own, and it decides which changes cannot wait. Changes are queued for the next release unless they correct a safety, accuracy or compliance defect. A safety induction that still teaches a procedure that changed today has such a defect; a leadership module that could be sharper does not.

How small a team can run the loop across a portfolio of programs is not something the paper settles. Its case study for corporate learning (claim 6) is where that would be learned.

## Independent experts

You author the standard. The paper's notes on Specify say the same structure can represent "a course authored by an independent expert". Its closing section says an independent expert teaching a craft can use the same stages with lighter gates.

Deciding in writing what "able to centre clay" means, before you record the lesson, is stage 2. Checking that students can do it, not only watch it, is stage 5.

No rule supplies a gate. No source for independent teaching has yet been read for this site, so this section rests on the paper alone and lists no sector words. A gate can be as light as a second reader, where you have one.

One open question is yours in particular. The paper versions a program by cohort and releases changes between cohorts. Where students enrol at any time and work at their own pace, there are no cohorts, and how versioning works then is not yet answered.
