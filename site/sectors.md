This page says, for five settings, where the learning standard comes from and which of that setting's own words the EDLC's words translate to. It also says which obligations in that setting's rules map to a stage, in the paper's own account (§8). Every fact about a sector links to its source; where nothing has been verified, the page says so rather than guessing.

The paper's own grounding is Australian vocational education. Whether the model transfers to the other four settings is untested: the paper's own [research agenda](/research/) lists testing it in other sectors.

A gate can be as light as a second reader. How formal it is depends on where you work ([what a gate is](/lifecycle/#gates)).

## Schools

This section covers the Australian Curriculum F–10. Senior secondary courses run on state standards and calendars this site has not yet read.

The standard comes from above the school. The Australian Curriculum version 9.0 was approved on 1 April 2022 and is reviewed on a six-year cycle. States and territories implement it on their own timelines ([ACARA, *Curriculum*](https://www.acara.edu.au/curriculum)).

In New South Wales, NESA sets syllabuses for all schools in the state ([NSW Government, *NESA*](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa)). In Victoria, the VCAA's guidelines sit alongside the advice each school sector authority gives its schools ([VCAA, *Victorian Curriculum F–10*](https://www.vcaa.vic.edu.au/curriculum/foundation-10/Pages/default.aspx)).

So the standard a school designs against is national, state and sector-shaped at once. The state's timeline decides when a revised standard reaches your program. Your own terms and intakes set when a release goes out, which is the paper's rule on the calendar (§7).

In this sector's words: the curriculum has eight learning areas. Its content descriptions and achievement standards are "the focus for planning, programming, teaching and assessment" ([ACARA, *Learning areas*](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/help/learning-areas)). Teachers develop teaching and learning programs, and states and territories set reporting ([ACARA, *Planning, teaching, assessing and reporting*](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/help/f-10-curriculum-overview/planning--teaching--assessing-and-reporting)).

Read "learning standard" as the curriculum or the syllabus, and "program" as a teaching and learning program. The nearest words to "outcome descriptor" are content description and achievement standard.

Which obligations in a state's or a sector authority's requirements map to a stage is not yet established. This site has verified where the standard comes from, not what the state requires of a program before or after it is taught. Until that is read, treat every gate as yours to set.

## Vocational education and training

The standard is usually a unit of competency from a training package. Its outcome descriptors are the unit's elements and performance criteria, with the separate Assessment Requirements: performance evidence, knowledge evidence and assessment conditions. [TAEASS412 *Assess competence*](https://training.gov.au/TrainingComponentFiles/TAE/TAEASS412_R1.pdf), for example, has six elements and 28 performance criteria, and its [Assessment Requirements](https://training.gov.au/TrainingComponentFiles/TAE/TAEASS412_AssessmentRequirements_R1.pdf) are a document of their own.

Providers' rules are the *National Vocational Education and Training Regulator (Outcome Standards for Registered Training Organisations) Instrument 2025* ([Federal Register of Legislation, F2025L00354](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00354/latest/text)). It commenced on 1 July 2025.

In this sector's words: "training product", "assessment tool", "scope of registration", "validation".

Two of the instrument's obligations supply a step the paper describes, under two different names (§8). Standard 1.3(2)(b) requires that "assessment tools are reviewed prior to use". That pre-use **review** maps to stage 8, [Validate and release](/stages/validate-and-release/).

Standard 1.5 requires that every training product on the organisation's scope of registration is validated at least once every five years. It requires validation more often where the organisation becomes aware of risks, of changes to the training product, or of relevant feedback. Standard 1.5(2)(c) has risk decide which components are validated and how large a sample.

Who validates matters. Under Standard 1.5(2)(f) the outcome is "not solely determined by a person who has designed or delivered the training or assessment". That post-use **validation** maps to stage 10, [Improve](/stages/improve/).

Both Standards end the same way: the outcomes of review (1.3) and of validation (1.5) must inform changes. That return path is the instrument's own.

Passing either step in the EDLC's sense is not the same as meeting the Standard, and nothing on this site is a compliance tool. The paper's own guardrail is that compliance is not quality: passing an audit shows conformance, not that learners thrived.

## Higher education

Above the provider sits the *Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021*, read here as Compilation No. 2 of 14 July 2026 ([Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L00488/latest/text)). This site has read its Standard 5.3, on monitoring, review and improvement, and claims nothing about its other Standards.

Standard 5.3.1 sets the periodic review. It reads: "All accredited courses of study are subject to periodic (at least every seven years) comprehensive reviews that are overseen by peak academic governance processes and include external referencing or other benchmarking activities" ([compiled text](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L00488/2026-07-14/2026-07-14/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html)). TEQSA's [*Guidance note: Academic monitoring, review and improvement*](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/guidance-notes/guidance-note-academic-monitoring-review-and-improvement) explains that Standard; the obligation is the Standard's.

That periodic comprehensive review maps to stage 10, [Improve](/stages/improve/), as a periodic gate across many cohorts rather than one per cohort. The per-cohort evidence the paper gathers at stages 9 and 10 resembles the interim monitoring Standard 5.3.3 names. The two are not the same step.

In this sector's words: "accredited course of study", "peak academic governance", "external referencing".

Two questions are open. Where course approval and annual monitoring sit in the loop has not been read for this site. Nor has where a unit's standard sits within the accredited course.

The paper's notes on Specify say only that the standard's structure can represent a university subject. The paper plans releases against intake dates, but it was grounded in vocational education and says so. Its fit with a university's rhythm is what the paper's research agenda would test.

## Workplace and soft skills

Often no outside body sets the standard. The organisation adopts one or writes its own. The paper's second stage, Specify, takes in *or authors* the standard, and its first principle allows a standard "authored in-house".

Three published frameworks an organisation might adopt:

- the *Core Skills for Work Developmental Framework* (2013): three skill clusters, ten skill areas, five stages of performance ([DEWR](https://www.dewr.gov.au/node/8374));
- SFIA 9 (October 2024): seven levels of responsibility ([SFIA Foundation](https://sfia-online.org/en/sfia-9));
- the *Australian Core Skills Framework* (2012), which the ACSF describes as five core skills ([ACER](https://people.acer.org/en/publications/australian-core-skills-framework-acsf/)).

In these frameworks' words: "skill cluster", "skill area", "stage of performance", "level of responsibility", "core skill".

No rule that supplies a gate has been read for this site, so every gate is yours to set. Whether rules such as those for safety training supply one is not yet known. The paper says gates can be lighter here: regulation sets how strict a gate is, not whether the stage exists.

One rule is the paper's own, and it decides which changes cannot wait. Changes are queued for the next release unless they correct a safety, accuracy or compliance defect. A safety induction that still teaches a procedure that changed today has such a defect; a leadership module that could be sharper does not.

How small a team can run the loop across a portfolio of programs is not something the paper settles. Its research agenda names a case study in corporate learning as where that would be learned.

## Independent experts

You author the standard. The paper's notes on Specify say the same structure can represent "a course authored by an independent expert". Its implications (§8) say an independent expert teaching a craft can use the same stages with lighter gates.

Deciding in writing what "able to centre clay" means, before you record the lesson, is stage 2. Checking that students can do it, not only watch it, is stage 5.

No rule that supplies a gate has been read for this site. No source for independent teaching has been read either, so this section rests on the paper alone and lists no sector words. A gate can be as light as a second reader, where you have one.

One open question is yours in particular. The paper versions a program by cohort and releases changes between cohorts. Where students enrol at any time and work at their own pace there are no cohorts, and how versioning works then is unanswered.
